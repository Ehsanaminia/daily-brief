# Curriculum v4 (27 Nov 2026): beginner to asset manager, risk manager and CEO

Version 4, after critic round 1 (report: `CRITIC_REPORT_curriculum_v4.md`, 49 findings; all FIX/ADD/MOVE items applied, see the change log at the end).
Built from: CFA Program Level I 2027 topic outline, Level III 2027 core and pathway outlines (CFA Institute), Level II 2026 topic outline (CFA Institute; module titles cross-checked with prep providers), GARP FRM 2026 Study Guide (Part I and II), plus desk practice, EU fund rules and firm leadership.

## Format (Ehsan, 27 Nov): three 10-minute episodes each weekday
| Slot | When | Content |
|---|---|---|
| A | Way to work | New lesson |
| B | Way to the gym | New lesson (always new content; often the applied side: real example with real data, case, "how to buy and sell it") |
| C | In the gym | Mon and Wed: third new lesson. Tue and Thu: review (market headlines from News Lab data, quiz on today + 1 week ago + 1 month ago, one interview question) |
Friday: A = week review and vocabulary sprint, B = weekly Myth vs Math report and growth ideas, C = mock interview (5 questions).
**10 new lessons a week** (Ehsan, 27 Nov: A and B Mon to Thu, plus C on Mon and Wed). The "weeks" below are blocks of 8 lessons, so the course moves faster than the block numbers. Each lesson: plain definition, real-life example, real data where it exists (dated, sourced), one "what a pro checks", quiz and interview questions. Math lessons get a free one-page formula card in the show notes.
Ethics runs as a strand: one Standard or case every few weeks, not a single block.

## Phase 0. Intro (week 1, done)
Asset manager, stock, bond, funds and ETFs, review.

## Phase 1. Foundations (weeks 2 to 13)
**W2 Money and time** [L1]: 1 Interest. 2 Compounding. 3 Inflation and real returns. 4 Present and future value. 5 Discount rates. 6 Annuities and perpetuities (mortgage example). 7 Return types: holding period, annualized, arithmetic vs geometric. 8 Benchmarks: what to compare a return with; money- vs time-weighted return.

**W3 Statistics I** [L1, F1]: 1 Average, median, spread. 2 Standard deviation: volatility, first look. 3 Distributions: binomial, normal, lognormal. 4 Fat tails, skew, kurtosis. 5 Probability and expected value. 6 Covariance and correlation. 7 Samples, standard error, central limit theorem, confidence intervals. 8 Hypothesis tests and p-values.

**W4 Statistics II** [L1, L2, F1]: 1 Simple regression and beta. 2 Correlation is not causation. 3 Percent vs percentage points vs basis points. 4 Reading a chart and a table honestly. 5 Monte Carlo simulation. 6 Bootstrapping. 7 Why most backtests lie (overfitting, survivorship, look-ahead). 8 Review lesson: statistics on one page.

**W5 Risk and return** [L1, F1]: 1 Risk premium. 2 Diversification math. 3 Efficient frontier. 4 CAPM and beta. 5 Sharpe, Treynor, Sortino, M-squared. 6 Factor models and APT. 7 Market efficiency: EMH and anomalies. 8 VaR and expected shortfall: first look.

**W6 Clients, minds and ethics I** [L1]: 1 Behavioral biases I: loss aversion, overconfidence. 2 Biases II: herding, anchoring, mental accounting. 3 The investment policy statement (IPS) in 10 minutes. 4 Risk tolerance: ability vs willingness. 5 Ethics: why trust is the product. 6 CFA Code and Standards overview. 7 Standard I: professionalism. 8 Standard II: integrity of markets (insider information, manipulation).

**W7 How markets really work** [L1, F1, Desk]: 1 Primary vs secondary markets, IPOs. 2 Exchanges, OTC, dark pools. 3 Brokers, dealers, market makers. 4 Order types and the order book. 5 Bid-ask, liquidity, market impact. 6 Short selling, margin, leverage. 7 Life of a trade: clearing, settlement (US T+1 since 28 May 2024; EU, UK, Switzerland move to T+1 on 11 Oct 2027), custody, corporate actions. 8 Trading costs and taxes.

**W8 Economics I** [L1, L2]: 1 Supply, demand and market structures. 2 GDP, jobs and the business cycle. 3 Long-run growth: labour, capital, productivity. 4 Inflation: CPI, PCE, HICP (the ECB's target), core. 5 Central banks and policy rates. 6 QE and QT. 7 Fiscal policy, deficits, government debt. 8 Reading a data release on the day.

**W9 Economics II and the players** [L1, F1]: 1 Trade, capital flows, balance of payments. 2 Geopolitics and markets. 3 Banks: how they make money. 4 Insurers and pension funds. 5 Asset managers, funds, ETF creation and redemption. 6 Hedge funds, private equity, sovereign funds, endowments. 7 Regulators: SEC, CFTC, ESMA, ECB/SSM, BaFin, FCA, PRA, IOSCO, FSB. 8 The EU rulebook: UCITS, AIFMD, MiFID II, SFDR, PRIIPs.

**W10 Reading company accounts I** [L1]: 1 Why statements. 2 Income statement. 3 Balance sheet. 4 Cash flow statement. 5 How the three link. 6 Inventories, long-term assets, depreciation. 7 Liabilities, leases, pensions. 8 Income taxes and deferred tax.

**W11 Reading company accounts II** [L1, L2]: 1 Profitability ratios. 2 Liquidity and solvency ratios. 3 Efficiency and DuPont. 4 Earnings quality and red flags (Enron, Wirecard). 5 IFRS vs US GAAP. 6 Forecasting statements. 7 Subsidiaries, multinationals, share-based pay. 8 Reading a bank's and an insurer's accounts.

**W12 Corporate finance** [L1, L2]: 1 Company forms and stakeholders. 2 Governance and agency. 3 Working capital. 4 Capital budgeting: NPV and IRR. 5 Cost of capital: WACC. 6 Capital structure and leverage. 7 Dividends and buybacks. 8 M&A and restructuring.

**W13 Ethics II and why risk management exists** [L1, L3, F1]: 1 Standard III: duties to clients (fair dealing, suitability). 2 Standard IV: duties to employers. 3 Standard V: analysis, recommendations, records. 4 Standard VI: conflicts of interest. 5 Standard VII: responsibilities as a candidate. 6 GARP Code of Conduct. 7 Why risk management exists: Barings in 10 minutes. 8 Ethics cases: front-running, trade allocation, soft dollars.

## Phase 2. Instruments and asset classes (weeks 14 to 39)
**W14 Cash and money markets** [L1, F1]: 1 The risk-free rate. 2 Treasury bills. 3 Commercial paper and CDs. 4 Repo and reverse repo. 5 Money market funds (2008 "breaking the buck"; March 2020). 6 Reference rates: SOFR, €STR, SONIA, EURIBOR; LIBOR ended 30 Sep 2024. 7 Bank deposits and funding. 8 Holding cash well: pre-trade checklist.

**W15 Derivatives I: forwards and futures** [L1, F1]: 1 What derivatives are, uses and risks. 2 Exchange vs OTC, central clearing. 3 Margin and marking to market. 4 Arbitrage, replication, cost of carry. 5 Pricing forwards. 6 Futures vs forwards. 7 Hedge ratio and basis risk. 8 Index futures: changing a portfolio's beta.

**W16 Bonds I: features and pricing** [L1]: 1 Features, indenture, covenants. 2 Cash flow types: zero, coupon, amortizing, floating. 3 Issuance and trading: auctions, dealers. 4 Government bond markets: Treasuries, Bunds, gilts. 5 Price and yield to maturity. 6 Clean vs dirty price, accrued interest, day counts. 7 Simple spreads: the G-spread over government bonds. 8 Floating-rate notes and discount margin.

**W17 Bonds II: rate risk** [L1, F1]: 1 Spot, par and forward curves. 2 Z-spread over the spot curve. 3 Yield curve shapes and theories. 4 Macaulay and modified duration. 5 Convexity. 6 DV01 and hedging with bond futures (cheapest to deliver). 7 Key rate duration, non-parallel shifts. 8 Short-rate models in plain words (Vasicek idea).

**W18 Bonds III: credit** [L1, L2, F1]: 1 PD, LGD, expected loss. 2 Ratings and agencies. 3 Sovereign and country risk. 4 Corporate credit analysis. 5 Investment grade vs high yield. 6 Default, recovery, seniority. 7 Credit spreads through the cycle. 8 Bank capital in plain words: AT1, CoCos and Credit Suisse 2023 (AT1 write-down about CHF 16 bn).

**W19 Options I** [L1, L3, F1]: 1 Calls and puts, payoffs. 2 Intrinsic and time value, moneyness. 3 Put-call parity. 4 European vs American. 5 One-period binomial model. 6 Covered call, protective put, collar. 7 Spreads: bull, bear, butterfly. 8 Straddles and strangles.

**W20 Bonds IV: structures and embedded options** [L1, L2, F1]: 1 Securitization. 2 ABS. 3 MBS and prepayment. 4 CMBS, CLOs, CDOs and 2008. 5 Callable, putable, convertible bonds. 6 Rate trees and OAS. 7 Credit models: Merton (equity as a call) and reduced form. 8 Inflation-linked bonds and break-even inflation.

**W21 Swaps and credit derivatives** [L1, L2, F1]: 1 Interest rate swaps, swap rates and the I-spread. 2 Swap valuation. 3 Currency swaps. 4 Caps, floors, swaptions. 5 Equity and total return swaps (Archegos). 6 Credit default swaps and CDS indices. 7 Counterparty risk and CVA, first look. 8 ISDA, collateral, clearing.

**W22 Bond portfolios** [L3, PM]: 1 Roles of fixed income. 2 Liability-driven investing and immunization. 3 Curve strategies: bullet, barbell, roll-down. 4 Credit strategies. 5 Bond indices and passive bond funds. 6 Emerging-market and municipal debt. 7 Case: UK LDI 2022 (Bank of England gilt purchases 28 Sep to 14 Oct 2022). 8 Buying and selling bonds: pre-trade.

**W23 Equities I** [L1]: 1 Share features, classes, voting. 2 Issuance: IPO, follow-on, buybacks. 3 Index construction. 4 Sources of equity returns. 5 Industry analysis: Porter, PESTLE. 6 Company analysis and business models. 7 Cyclical vs defensive sectors. 8 Developed vs emerging markets.

**W24 Equities II: valuation** [L1, L2]: 1 Intrinsic vs market value, and the cost of equity you discount with. 2 Dividend discount and Gordon growth. 3 Free cash flow (FCFF, FCFE). 4 DCF in practice, terminal value traps. 5 P/E, P/B, P/S. 6 EV/EBITDA. 7 Residual income. 8 Private company valuation.

**W25 Equities III: return drivers** [L1, L2, Desk]: 1 Multiple regression and its traps (refresher for factor models). 2 Equity risk premium. 3 Multifactor models (Fama-French, Carhart). 4 Styles: value, growth, quality, momentum, low volatility. 5 Research reports: sell side vs buy side. 6 Activists and short sellers. 7 ESG in analysis. 8 Earnings season and how prices react.

**W26 Equity portfolios** [L3, PM]: 1 Passive investing and tracking error. 2 Smart beta. 3 Active: fundamental vs quant; the fundamental law (IR ≈ IC × √breadth). 4 Long/short and market neutral. 5 Position sizing and concentration. 6 Small caps and liquidity. 7 Stock lending. 8 Buying and selling stocks: pre-trade.

**W27 to W28 Real estate and real assets** [L1, L2, PMk]: 1 Direct property vs REITs. 2 NOI and cap rates. 3 Valuation: income, comparables, cost. 4 Financing: LTV, DSCR. 5 Property cycles. 6 Listed vs private, appraisal smoothing. 7 REIT analysis: FFO, AFFO. 8 Commercial vs residential. 9 Infrastructure. 10 Farmland, timberland, natural resources. 11 Case: 2008 housing. 12 Case: the office slump after 2020. 13 Real assets and inflation. 14 Getting real estate exposure: pre-trade. 15 Interview workshop. 16 Review.

**W29 to W30 Commodities and gold** [L1, L2, F1]: 1 Physical vs futures. 2 Oil and OPEC. 3 Gas and power. 4 Metals. 5 Agriculture. 6 Gold and central banks. 7 Futures curves: contango and backwardation. 8 Roll yield. 9 Commodity indices. 10 Return decomposition. 11 Commodities and inflation. 12 Case: 20 Apr 2020, expiring May WTI future settles at minus 37.63 dollars (Brent stays positive). 13 Trading commodities: pre-trade. 14 Interview workshop. 15 to 16 Review and data practice.

**W31 to W32 Currencies** [L1, L2, L3, F1]: 1 Quotes and cross rates. 2 Forward points. 3 Covered interest parity. 4 Uncovered parity and carry. 5 PPP. 6 Balance of payments and FX. 7 Regimes and pegs. 8 Crises: 1992, 1997, 2015 Swiss franc. 9 FX in a portfolio. 10 Hedging decisions and hedge ratios. 11 Cost of hedging. 12 Emerging-market currencies. 13 How FX trades: spot, forwards, NDFs. 14 Currency management [L3]. 15 Interview workshop. 16 Review.

**W33 Options II: the Greeks** [L2, F1]: 1 Black-Scholes-Merton: the idea. 2 BSM: the inputs and what each does. 3 Delta. 4 Gamma. 5 Vega. 6 Theta and rho. 7 Delta hedging and gamma scalping. 8 Exotics: barrier, Asian, digital.

**W34 Volatility** [F1, F2, Desk]: 1 Realized vs implied. 2 Time series and measuring volatility: EWMA, GARCH. 3 The VIX. 4 Smile and skew. 5 Term structure and surface. 6 Variance swaps, volatility as an asset. 7 Case: 5 Feb 2018 (VIX up about 20 points, biggest one-day rise since 1987; XIV lost 84% and was terminated). 8 Reading the volatility market daily.

**W35 Derivatives in portfolios** [L3]: 1 Options strategies for managers. 2 Swaps, forwards and futures strategies. 3 Overlays. 4 Tail hedging. 5 Portable alpha. 6 Currency overlays. 7 Case: a pension hedging program. 8 Pre-trade checklist for derivatives.

**W36 Digital assets** [L1, F2]: 1 Blockchain. 2 Bitcoin. 3 Ethereum and smart contracts. 4 Stablecoins. 5 Custody and exchanges (FTX). 6 Crypto ETFs and ETPs. 7 Tokenization. 8 Regulation: MiCA (stablecoin rules from 30 Jun 2024, fully from 30 Dec 2024) and portfolio role.

**W37 to W38 Private markets** [L1, PMk, F2]: GP/LP structures and terms, investment process, buyouts and LBO math, venture and growth, private debt and direct lending, special situations and distressed, private real estate, infrastructure funds, J-curve, IRR vs TVPI and DPI, valuation and smoothing, secondaries and co-investments, fees and carry, due diligence, private credit risks today, review.

**W39 Hedge funds** [L1, L2, F2]: 1 Equity long/short. 2 Global macro. 3 Event-driven and merger arbitrage. 4 Relative value; LTCM 1998. 5 Managed futures (CTAs). 6 Multi-strategy pods. 7 Fees, leverage, liquidity terms. 8 Due diligence and replication.

## Phase 3. How assets interact (weeks 40 to 43)
**W40 Interactions**: correlations that change, stock-bond correlation regimes, the dollar, real rates and gold, oil and inflation, credit vs equity, risk-on/risk-off, liquidity and central banks.
**W41 Crises**: 1987, 1998, 2000, 2008, March 2020 (dash for cash), 2022, 2023 (SVB 10 Mar; Credit Suisse 19 Mar), what diversification failed.
**W42 to W43 Capital market expectations** [L3, L2]: framework, macro inputs, growth and potential GDP, forecasting bond, equity, real estate and currency returns, regimes, building a multi-asset view, a full worked example, review.

## Phase 4. Risk management (weeks 44 to 50) [FRM]
**W44 Foundations** [F1]: governance, risk appetite, three lines, ERM, data aggregation and reporting, credit risk transfer, risk-adjusted performance, the 2007 to 2009 crisis.
**W45 Bank capital and regulation** [F1, F2]: Basel I and II, Basel III (capital, leverage), economic vs regulatory capital, RAROC, stress testing basics, climate risk as a financial risk, review.
**W46 Market risk** [F1, F2]: VaR three ways (lesson each), expected shortfall and coherence, backtesting, extreme value theory, stress tests and scenarios, correlation and copulas, FRTB.
**W47 Credit risk** [F2]: expected and unexpected loss, credit VaR, portfolio credit, counterparty risk and xVA (two lessons), securitization risk, bank stress testing.
**W48 Operational risk and resilience** [F2]: framework, cyber, third parties and DORA (EU, from 17 Jan 2025), fraud and AML, model risk and validation, business continuity, op-risk capital, review.
**W49 Liquidity and treasury risk** [F2]: funding vs market liquidity, LCR and NSFR, liquidity stress tests, contingency funding, funds transfer pricing, ALM, fund liquidity tools (swing pricing, gates, notice periods); cases Northern Rock and SVB.
**W50 Risk in investment management** [F2]: factor risk models, risk budgeting, active risk and information ratio, risk monitoring, due diligence, asset liquidity, current issues (AI, private credit, geopolitics, government debt).

## Phase 5. Portfolio management (weeks 51 to 55) [L3 core, PM]
**W51 Clients**: IPS in depth, pensions, endowments, SWFs, insurers, private wealth and goals-based planning, LDI.
**W52 Asset allocation**: strategic allocation, mean-variance limits, Black-Litterman, risk parity, tactical allocation, rebalancing, constraints and taxes, alternatives.
**W53 Trading and execution**: microstructure, algorithms (VWAP, TWAP), transaction cost analysis, best execution (MiFID II), electronic markets, a day on a trading desk.
**W54 Performance**: return attribution, risk attribution, appraisal measures, GIPS, manager selection, fees.
**W55 Cases and the Asset Manager Code**: sovereign fund, pension fund, family office, endowment, Asset Manager Code, Standards application cases.

## Phase 6. Quant and modern tools (week 56) [L1, L2, F1]
Machine learning in finance, big and alternative data, AI and LLMs in investing, Python and spreadsheets for analysts, data sources, backtesting done right.

## Phase 7. Leading a firm (weeks 57 to 58) [Lead]
Economics of asset management (AUM, fees, margins, passive shift), distribution and client service, product design, investment committees, risk culture and compliance, people and talent, technology and operations, M&A among managers, board and governance, crisis leadership, founder case studies.

## Phase 8. Career and exams (weeks 59 to 60)
CFA and FRM paths and how this course maps to them, roles and firm types, stock and macro pitches, technical and behavioral questions, CV and LinkedIn, full mock interviews, a 12-month plan.

## Size and honesty
- About 430 new lessons (some later blocks are listed by theme and get their full lessons when planned). At 10 a week that is about 43 weeks; with 4 to 6 buffer weeks (holidays, big market days) it ends around November 2027.
- A 10-minute episode builds understanding and vocabulary. It does not replace exam study: CFA Institute says successful candidates report spending over 300 hours on average per level, and GARP's 2026 candidate guide says candidates spend about 275 hours studying on average. The podcast is the companion; books and question banks are the exam prep.
- Scope: the CFA Level III Portfolio Management and Private Markets pathways are covered in depth; the Private Wealth pathway only in W51.
- Facts that go stale (rules, dates, rates) are re-checked before each episode and said "as of".
